# Destination Planning Framework

Use this knowledge when building destination recommendations. Treat it as general guidance; current entry rules, closures, fares, weather warnings and local restrictions must be verified from live sources.

## Destination selection factors
- Trip purpose: leisure, business, family, honeymoon, adventure, culture, food, nature, shopping, or mixed.
- Duration: distinguish short city breaks from multi-city trips.
- Budget: separate transport, accommodation, food, activities, local transport, taxes, insurance and contingency.
- Season: consider temperature, rainfall, snow, daylight, peak season and local festivals.
- Travel style: relaxed, balanced, packed, luxury, budget, backpacking, family-friendly.
- Accessibility: walking requirements, elevators, public transport, step-free access and room accessibility.
- Connectivity: airport access, rail/bus availability and transfer time.

## Itinerary principles
- Avoid changing hotels every night unless the route strongly benefits from it.
- Group nearby attractions together geographically.
- Leave buffer time for airport transfers, queues, meals and unexpected delays.
- Put time-sensitive attractions on the appropriate day and avoid overloading the first day after a long flight.
- For multi-city trips, compare open-jaw flights, rail and point-to-point options.
- Prefer realistic daily plans over maximum attraction counts.

## Agentic planning implication
A planner should first identify constraints, then generate candidates, retrieve destination knowledge when useful, call live MCP tools for flights/hotels/weather, validate feasibility and replan when constraints conflict.
